## SECTION 2 — SMALL-CAP SPECIFIC FINANCIAL HEALTH

For each stock, assess through a small-cap lens (small-caps fail differently than 
large-caps — liquidity, promoter behavior, and earnings consistency matter most):

**A. Earnings Quality & Consistency**
- Revenue CAGR: 3-year. Is growth consistent quarter-to-quarter or lumpy?
- EBITDA margin: Expanding or compressing? At what utilization level?
- PAT margin: Normalize for one-time items.
- **Red Flag Test:** Any year in the last 3 where PAT was negative or fell > 20%? 
  Flag immediately.
- Earnings surprise track record: Has the company beaten consensus estimates 
  in the last 4 quarters? (Use Trendlyne / Screener.in data)

**B. Balance Sheet — Small-Cap Stress Test**
- Debt-to-equity: For small-caps, D/E > 0.75 is a caution signal (less headroom 
  than large-caps in a credit tightening cycle).
- Cash & equivalents vs short-term debt: Can the company survive a 6-month 
  revenue shock without refinancing?
- Receivables quality: Debtor days trending up = potential revenue recognition 
  issue — flag for small-caps especially.

**C. Promoter Quality — Non-Negotiable for Small-Caps**
- Promoter holding %: Ideally > 50%. Trend — is it increasing or decreasing?
- Promoter pledge %: Flag if > 10%. Above 25% is a serious red flag in small-caps.
- Management guidance track record: Compare last 3 years of guidance vs actuals. 
  A promoter who consistently over-promises is a structural risk.
- Related party transactions: Any large or unusual RPTs? Flag for investigation.
- Salary/remuneration vs PAT ratio: Flag if promoter salary > 5% of PAT 
  (signals extraction over value creation).

**D. Free Cash Flow — The Acid Test**
- OCF/PAT ratio: Above 0.8 = strong earnings quality.
  Below 0.5 for 2+ consecutive years = earnings may be accounting-driven, not real.
- FCF yield (FCF / Market Cap): Rank all 5 holdings by this metric. 
  Negative FCF for a small-cap in growth mode is acceptable only if capex is 
  clearly productive (rising asset turns).
- Capex cycle: Is the company investing for future growth or maintaining 
  existing assets? Important for return trajectory.

## SECTION 3 — VALUATION WITH SMALL-CAP CONTEXT

Small-caps carry a liquidity discount and execution risk premium. 
Valuation must account for this:

**A. Relative Valuation**
- Trailing P/E vs: (a) own 3-year average, (b) listed peers, (c) sector median
- EV/EBITDA vs peers
- Build peer comparison table for each stock:
  | Metric    | [Stock] | Peer 1 | Peer 2 | Sector Median |
  
**B. Intrinsic Value Estimate**
- Compute 2-year forward EPS estimate using: Base EPS × (1 + growth rate)²
- Apply a justified P/E multiple. For small-caps, the P/E multiple should reflect:
  - Growth rate (higher growth = higher multiple, but cap at reasonable levels)
  - ROCE quality (ROCE > 20% justifies premium)
  - Liquidity discount: Apply 10–15% discount to fair value vs comparable 
    mid-cap peers to account for lower liquidity
- State fair value as a range: Conservative / Base / Optimistic case

**C. Satellite Justification Test**
For each stock, answer: 
"At the current price, does this stock offer enough return potential 
to justify the higher risk vs simply buying a Nifty Smallcap 250 Index Fund?"
- If expected 3-year return < 15% CAGR: HARD TO JUSTIFY as direct holding
- If expected 3-year return 15–20% CAGR: ACCEPTABLE with strong fundamentals
- If expected 3-year return > 20% CAGR: STRONG SATELLITE CANDIDATE
